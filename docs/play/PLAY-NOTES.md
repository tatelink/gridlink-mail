# Google Play build: permissions, declarations and console notes

Written 2026-11-07 on branch `play-flavor`, from the merged manifest of the `playRelease`
variant (versionName 2.1.2, versionCode 1005, targetSdk 36). Nothing here has been entered in
Play Console. Re-check this list against the merged manifest of whatever build is uploaded:
`app/build/intermediates/merged_manifests/playRelease/processPlayReleaseManifest/AndroidManifest.xml`.

## How the Play build differs from the F-Droid build

Same applicationId (`app.gridlink`), versionCode, signing key and features. The only difference
is the tip jar in Settings: Play Billing tips (`tip_small`, `tip_medium`, `tip_large`) in place of
the Ko-fi link. Diffing the two merged manifests, the Play build adds exactly:

- the permission `com.android.vending.BILLING`
- `ProxyBillingActivity` and `ProxyBillingActivityV2` (billing library, not exported)
- `GoogleApiActivity` and the `com.google.android.gms.version` meta-data (Play services base)
- Google datatransport: `TransportBackendDiscovery`, `JobInfoSchedulerService`,
  `AlarmManagerSchedulerBroadcastReceiver` (all not exported) and the CCT backend meta-data
- package-visibility `<queries>` for the Play Store billing service and Google's billing test
  companion

Libraries the Play build adds (all through billing-ktx 9.1.0): billing, play-services-base,
basement, location, places-placereport, tasks, datatransport (api, runtime, backend-cct),
firebase-encoders (core, json, proto), javax.inject. `play-services-location` comes in as a
transitive dependency of billing but adds NO location permission; the merged manifest has no
`ACCESS_*_LOCATION`.

## Declarations Play Console will ask for

### 1. Foreground service permissions (required, the known one)

Console path: App content, Foreground service permissions. Play asks which FGS permission, the
task it performs, the user impact if deferred or interrupted, and a link to a short video of the
feature in use. Draft answers:

- **Permission:** `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE`
- **Feature / task:**
  > Gridlink Mail delivers new-mail notifications in real time from the user's own mail
  > servers. While an account has new-mail notifications on, a foreground service holds open
  > the push connection the user's server offers: a JMAP EventSource stream or an IMAP IDLE
  > session (the default account, or every account when the user turns on "Push for all
  > accounts"). The service shows a persistent, user-visible notification while it runs. The
  > user can turn notifications off per account in Settings, after which that account holds no
  > connection, and the service stops when no account needs one.
- **Why no other type or API fits:**
  > Gridlink connects to self-hosted and third-party mail servers that the user chooses. Those
  > servers cannot send Firebase Cloud Messaging messages, so FCM is not an alternative, and
  > there is no Google-provided push channel for IMAP or JMAP. The `dataSync` type does not fit:
  > nothing is being transferred while the connection waits, the connection must stay open
  > indefinitely, and `dataSync` is time-limited on Android 15. The work cannot be deferred to
  > WorkManager because a deferred check is exactly the delay that realtime push exists to
  > remove. Accounts the user routes through a UnifiedPush distributor hold no connection
  > here.
- **User impact if the task were deferred or interrupted:**
  > New mail would arrive late, only at the next periodic check (minutes to hours, depending on
  > Android's background limits), instead of within seconds. The user left notifications
  > on to be told about new mail when it arrives.
- **Video:** a 30 to 60 second screen recording: with an account added and notifications on,
  the persistent notification is showing; a mail sent from another device triggers a
  notification within seconds; turning that account's notifications off in Settings removes
  the persistent notification. Needs recording on a phone with a real account (not by an
  agent on Brandon's phones).

Checked against `PushService.reconnectAll` on 2026-11-07: watched accounts are the default one
(or all, with "Push for all accounts"), minus accounts with notifications off, accounts served
by UnifiedPush, and accounts whose login the server refused; with none left the service stops.

### 2. Data safety form (required for every app)

Facts from the code, for filling it in:

- No analytics, no ads, no crash reporting, no server of ours. Mail, contacts and calendar data
  go only between the device and the servers the user configured.
- The contacts and calendar mirror copies the user's own CardDAV/CalDAV data into the system
  providers on the device; device contacts are read for recipient suggestions and stay on the
  device.
- Play Billing: purchases are handled by Google Play. The billing library itself sends Google
  its own diagnostics through Google datatransport (the CCT backend in the merged manifest).
  Google publishes what the billing library collects for the Data safety form; that list should
  be copied from Google's current page when filling the form (not verifiable offline here).
- The app keeps only a local count of completed tips, on the device, for the thank-you line.

How to answer "data collected" for the user's own mail is a judgement call, see Decisions.

### 3. Other App content items

- **Privacy policy URL:** required (the app handles contacts, calendar and email). No policy URL
  was found in the repo.
- **App access:** the app is unusable without a mail account, so reviewers need working test
  credentials (server, username, password) for a demo account, or the review will fail.
- **Ads:** no ads.
- **Content rating questionnaire, target audience (not children), news app (no):** standard.
- **Financial features, health, government, VPN, accessibility:** none apply.

### 4. In-app products (needed before the tip jar shows anything)

Create three **one-time, consumable** products with exactly these ids: `tip_small`,
`tip_medium`, `tip_large`, and activate them. Prices are set there; the app shows whatever Play
returns and orders the pills cheapest first. Until they exist (or on a device without the Play
Store) the card stays and says quietly that tips are not available, with no buttons.
Play Console only allows creating products after a build with the BILLING permission has been
uploaded to some track, and needs the Eight Point Labs payments profile set up.

Suggested product titles and descriptions (Play shows them on its purchase sheet):

- `tip_small`: "Small tip" / "A small thank-you for Gridlink. Unlocks nothing."
- `tip_medium`: "Medium tip" / "A thank-you for Gridlink. Unlocks nothing."
- `tip_large`: "Large tip" / "A big thank-you for Gridlink. Unlocks nothing."

## Build notes

- Build: `./gradlew :app:bundlePlayRelease`, output
  `app/build/outputs/bundle/playRelease/app-play-release.aab`, signed with the release key from
  `keystore.properties` (same certificate as the F-Droid and GitHub builds, SHA-256
  `17fac1d9...2dca96`). With Play App Signing, this key becomes the upload key unless a separate
  upload key is generated.
- The AAB carries the R8 mapping (`BUNDLE-METADATA/.../proguard.map`), so Play's crash reports
  can be deobfuscated.
- `dependenciesInfo.includeInBundle = false` is kept from the F-Droid setup, so the AAB carries
  no dependency metadata. Play Console will show a notice about it, and Play's SDK Index checks
  for known-bad SDK versions cannot run. Turning it on affects only the AAB, never the F-Droid
  APK (`includeInApk` stays false). Left as is pending a decision.
- The Play-only strings (`app/src/play/res/values/strings.xml`) are English only; other
  locales show English in the tip card.
