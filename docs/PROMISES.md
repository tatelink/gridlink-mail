# What Gridlink Mail promises

These are not aims. Each one is something the app already does. A shorter version of this text is in
the app, under **Settings → About → What this app promises**,
because a promise you have to visit a repo to read is not a promise made to the person holding the
phone. The two copies are meant to say the same thing and are changed together.

## Your gestures stay where they are

Swipes, taps and shortcuts you have learned stay put. A redesign does not get to move them out from
under your thumb, and nothing is reassigned unless you choose it.

Swipe actions, thread actions and the message-list layout are all settings, and a release that wants
a different default does not get to reach into the ones you have set.

## Problems get a straight answer

Bugs and requests go to the feedback address in About, and they reach a person, not a form or a
queue.

There is no telemetry, so that address is the only route a problem has. It is one person reading it,
which is why the promise is about where a report lands rather than about how fast it is answered.

## Nothing on this phone is behind a payment

Every feature that runs on your device is free, now and later. No paid tier, no unlock, no
advertising.

This one has teeth: it rules out the usual route where a client ships free, grows an audience, and
then puts a lock on the feature that audience came for. Anything that runs locally is yours. There
is a support link in About, but it is only a link: nothing prompts you toward it, and nothing in the
app behaves differently whether you use it or not.

## Nothing is collected about you

No analytics, no crash reporting, no identifiers. Nothing about how you use this app leaves your
phone. The feedback address in About is the only route to anyone, and only if you write to it.

That address exists *because* there is no telemetry: with nothing reporting home, a problem reaches
a human only if you tell someone, so the app makes that easy instead of watching you.

## Nothing is sent in your name without asking

The app does not answer mail for you. When a sender asks to be told you read their message, nothing
goes back unless you tap **Send receipt**, and there is no setting that turns automatic sending on.

Reading a message is not consent to tell the sender you read it. A setting that could send one
automatically would be a setting somebody else holding your phone could flip, so it does not exist.

## The licence travels with the app

The whole app is under the GPL, and you can read the full licence text in About without leaving the
app. Every promise here is about how this app behaves on your phone, not about anything happening
somewhere else.

That is the point of the list. Each one is written so it can fail visibly: a gesture that moved, a
receipt that went out on its own, a feature that grew a price.

The source is not published today, so one of these, the telemetry promise, is the one you cannot see
for yourself from the outside. It is stated here rather than left out, because a list of promises
that quietly omits the one you have to take on trust is worse than one that names it.
